Summary / Decision

Pulsar-WaW leads; the fleet breaks earlier than the simulator

We compared WaW and Pulsar-WaW, with PowerOf2 and round-robin as baselines, on a heterogeneous fleet of 20 backends with 2-20 GPUs each (220 GPUs). The discrete-event simulator and the five-region dev clusters ran the same workload, engine model and routing configuration.

Pulsar-WaW matched or beat WaW at every rate on both the simulator and the fleet. At 550 requests/s on the fleet it met the SLO for 100.0% of requests with 92.6% reuse; WaW reached 99.6% with 85.5% reuse and 148 timeouts.

Fleet / Matched against the simulator

The fleet agrees below saturation

Pulsar-WaW agrees with the simulator at 250 and 550 requests/s, and PowerOf2 at 250. At 250, Pulsar-WaW reached 249.6 goodput and 94.0% reuse on the fleet against 249.3 and 94.0% simulated; PowerOf2 reached 200.2 against 200.7.

Fleet goodput at 550 is about 5% below the simulator because fewer turns were offered: 520 requests/s for Pulsar-WaW against 550 simulated. Real network and streaming time lengthen each turn. Pulsar-WaW still met the SLO for 100.0% of what was offered.

Small backends are the pressure point. WaW's affinity ring gives each backend an equal share of sessions whatever its size. At 550 requests/s its 2-GPU backend ran at 7.0 s p50 on the fleet and 6.1 s in the simulator, and the 2-4 GPU backends produced all 148 of its fleet timeouts. Pulsar-WaW also slows its 2-GPU backend (2.1 s fleet, 2.5 s simulated) but completed 1,103 requests there in the measured window against 419 for WaW, with no timeouts.

Fleet / Small backends

WaW tips small backends into cache thrash

Under WaW the 2-GPU backend served the simulated behavior for three minutes, then switched state in minute 4: TTFT p50 rose to 2.5 s and reuse fell to 60%. It never recovered. Under Pulsar-WaW the same backend stayed at 0.21 s p50 or better with 93%-94% reuse.

This matches the bistable small-backend behavior first seen in the simulator: once evictions start, reuse falls, prefill work grows and the backend stays overloaded. The simulator stayed in the good state at 250 under both seeds; the fleet tipped over. The trigger is not isolated: both policies sent this backend about the same number of requests over the run (3,075 under Pulsar-WaW, 3,049 under WaW), so the difference is in how each policy reacts once the backend slows.

CPU is not the cause: the nodes hosting this backend peaked at 0.88 cores during the WaW measured window, on 2-vCPU nodes.

Conclusions / Evidence record

Ship candidate: Pulsar-WaW with the tested configuration

What the data supports

  • Pulsar-WaW (maximum-TPS weighting, 300 ms affinity wait, 300 ms band widening, max_queued 4, overflow only to free engine slots) is the better of the two shippable policies on both environments.
  • Small backends are WaW's weakness on a heterogeneous fleet: they overload first and, once they start evicting, do not recover within a run.
  • The simulator is a reliable guide below saturation. It is optimistic near saturation, where the fleet tips into collapse earlier.

Next measurements

  • Repeat 700 and 900 requests/s on larger MockDC nodes with Pylon CPU headroom and throttling counters, to separate infrastructure limits from routing behavior.
  • Add a second fleet seed and a 700 requests/s pair to locate the fleet collapse point.
  • Run round-robin and the 150 requests/s baselines on the fleet; they were skipped for time.
  • Repeat the decisive cases on real engines before a production recommendation.

Limitations

MockDynamo models batching, chunked prefill and per-worker KV caching with estimated step costs; it is not calibrated against a real engine. Cache reuse assumes a cached session covers its whole prompt; there is no block-level prefix matching. Load balancers use an unseeded random generator, so simulator runs are not bit-for-bit reproducible. Fleet CPU samples come from the kubelet summary API every 30 s.
