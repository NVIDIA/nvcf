Final report / Decision summary

Cache affinity helps until load breaks reuse

WaW is useful for cache-sensitive traffic below the observed pressure transition. It has not shown a consistent performance advantage over PowerOf2 across the tested workloads.

The maximum-TPS change improves Pulsar-WaW against its same-build mean control: throughput rises 2.45%, TTFT p99 falls 14.86%, and end-to-end p99 falls 12.00%. Failures and retries increase.

Five regions / Growing sessions

Higher low-load reuse; the high-load drop remains

Each column has its own matched PowerOf2 controls: two repeats per algorithm and cap, with reversed order. Both candidates use 768 sessions with 16 growing turns, cold reset and final drain. The two candidates ran on different dates.

At 160 RPS, one affine backend / 300 ms raises observed reuse to 88.0-89.0%, versus 68.5-70.3% for two / 200 ms. At 200-320, both candidates lose throughput to their matched PowerOf2 controls and retain only about 9-18% reuse.

Build A / Continuous fixed-prompt ramps

Cache reuse depends on the path through load

The 160 / 180 / 200 / 180 / 160 RPS sequence keeps sessions, caches and in-flight work across steps. WaW uses one affine backend and a 300 ms wait. Pulsar uses mean weighting and a different policy, including fixed 4,000 ms queue bounds.

Ordinary WaW reaches 96.75% settled reuse at opening 160, drops during upward 180, remains near 8% at 200 and returning 180, then recovers to 95.57% at 160. PowerOf2 stays near 5-6% reuse but has better aggregate throughput and latency tails.

The first mean-based Pulsar arm is worse overall than the reused controls. Its observed mean-TPS weight movement is a diagnostic lead, not a per-key trace or proof of a sole cause. Five aggregate windows cannot recover the exact transition timing.

Build B / Maximum-TPS tradeoff

The failure regression must stay visible

The same-build selector change improves successful throughput, input reuse and successful-request tails. It also increases terminal failures from 4 to 73 and retries from 8,183 to 13,389. Every terminal failure in this pair is HTTP 503.

What the pair supports

  • Maximum weighting sustains 88.61% input reuse in the settled upward 180 RPS window, versus 6.59% for mean.
  • The reuse drop still occurs at 200 RPS. It persists when returning to 180 and recovers at 160. The maximum signal delays this observed transition; it does not eliminate it.
  • p99 values cover successful requests only. Lower successful-request p99 together with more failures is a tradeoff, not a failure-aware SLO win.
  • One mean-then-maximum pair does not quantify run-to-run variability, order effects or a stable production threshold.

What it does not establish

There is no fresh build-B PowerOf2 or ordinary WaW control. Historical PowerOf2 is numerically better in aggregate throughput, tails and failures, but those values do not isolate the selector change. The preserved summaries also do not establish the underlying cause of the HTTP 503 regression.

Production recommendation remains pending real-engine QA. Keep the maximum-TPS result as a candidate improvement with an explicit reliability tradeoff.

Conclusions / Evidence record

Keep PowerOf2 as the benchmark and test the tradeoff

Next measurements, in order

  • Run a fresh same-build comparison of PowerOf2, ordinary WaW, Pulsar mean and Pulsar maximum. Use multiple matched blocks and reverse or randomize order.
  • Diagnose the HTTP 503 regression with request/attempt failure reasons, routing deadlines and backend errors. Include failed requests in SLO attainment.
  • Bracket the cache-reuse transition with finer load steps and longer plateaus around 180-200 RPS. Test cold-start and prewarmed runs separately; report achieved admissions.
  • Vary affine count, wait and working-set size separately. Record per-backend cache occupancy/evictions, queue depth, mean/max TPS and bounded owner/rank movement.
  • Repeat the decisive cases on real engines with true prefix caching and dynamic batching before a production recommendation.

Limits that change the interpretation

MockDynamo models an affinity-key/token-count LRU and fixed input/decode timing. It does not reproduce real prefix hashing or dynamic batching. Aggregate cache fit does not guarantee per-backend residency. Routing spill, duplication and eviction are plausible contributors; this study does not isolate their causal order.

No confidence intervals can be reconstructed. The fixed-duration, growing-session and continuous-ramp experiments have different workload, censoring and quantile semantics. Telemetry gaps cannot establish continuous healthy connectivity. Final connection checks were recorded at test completion; they are not a current cluster-health check.
